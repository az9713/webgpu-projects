Principal components without a decomposition kernel

There is no SVD and no eigen-solver among the 207 kernels, so the standard route is closed. Power iteration works anyway: multiply a vector by the covariance again and again and normalise, and it turns toward the leading direction. Deflate that direction away and repeat for the second. The counter shows how many multiplications it took, so slow convergence is visible rather than mysterious.

The science

Principal components are the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix C = XTX / n, computed after subtracting the mean from every column. The leading eigenvector points along the direction of greatest variance; its eigenvalue λ1 is that variance. The usual route is an eigendecomposition or an SVD, and neither exists among these kernels.

Power iteration reaches the same answer with nothing but multiplication. Write any starting vector in the eigenvector basis, v = Σ ciui. Multiplying by C scales each term by its own eigenvalue, so after k steps the vector is Σ ciλikui. Divide through by λ1k and every other term carries a factor (λi/λ1)k, which shrinks geometrically. Normalising after each multiply keeps the numbers finite; the direction converges on u1.

The rate depends on one number only, the ratio λ2/λ1. Reaching a tolerance ε takes roughly k ≈ log ε / log(λ2/λ1) iterations, so the count climbs steeply as the gap closes: the page needs 27 iterations at λ1/λ2 = 2 and 88 at 1.1, against a tolerance of 10−6. The exact constant also depends on how much of the random starting vector happened to lie along u1. When the two eigenvalues are equal there is no single answer to converge on, and the method never settles.

The second component comes from Hotelling deflation: subtract λ1u1u1T from C, which sets the found eigenvalue to zero and leaves the others untouched, then run the same iteration again.

Pipeline: ReduceMean and Sub centre the data; Gemm with transA builds the covariance XTX / n; each iteration is one MatMul and one LpNormalization; the deflation is Gemm for the outer product and Sub. The scores that are plotted are one last MatMul. Convergence stops when the vector moves by less than 10−6 between iterations. The rate is set by the ratio of the two largest eigenvalues: close eigenvalues converge slowly, and the slider lets you make them close.
