Take a floating point number apart

Every 32-bit float is one sign bit, eight exponent bits and twenty-three fraction bits. The GPU splits them with a BitCast to an unsigned integer, then shifts and masks. Type a number, then drag the slider and watch the exponent field fill up until exp(x) overflows to infinity.

The science

IEEE 754 binary32 spends its 32 bits on one sign, eight exponent bits and twenty-three fraction bits, and reads them as (−1)s × 2e−127 × (1 + f/223). The leading 1 is not stored; it is implied. That single trick buys a free bit of precision and is the reason the format is not simply a fixed-point number.

The consequence is that precision is relative, not absolute. The gap between neighbouring floats near a value x is about x · 2−23, so there are always about seven significant decimal digits, whether x is 10−10 or 1010. This is why adding a small number to a large one can change nothing at all.

The exponent field has two reserved values, and both are visible on this page. An exponent of 255 means infinity when the fraction is zero and NaN when it is not, which is why ∞ − ∞ and 0/0 produce a value that is not equal to itself. An exponent of 0 means a subnormal: the implied leading 1 is dropped and the value is 2−126 × f/223, which fills the gap between the smallest normal number, 1.18 × 10−38, and zero, down to 1.4 × 10−45. Subnormals cost silicon, and many GPUs simply flush them to zero; the last row divides 10−38 by 105 to land squarely inside that range and reports what this device chose.

The overflow is not a bug either. The largest finite float is about 3.403 × 1038, so exp(x) must overflow the moment x passes ln(3.403 × 1038) = 88.7228. Drag the slider and the exponent field fills to 255 at 88.73.

The decoded line is (−1)sign × 2exponent − 127 × (1 + fraction / 223). An exponent field of 255 means infinity when the fraction is zero and NaN otherwise. An exponent field of 0 is a subnormal: no hidden 1, and 2−126 instead. Many GPUs flush subnormals to zero, and the last row shows what this one does.
