The science

A seam is a connected path of pixels, one per row, that crosses the picture from top to bottom and never steps sideways by more than one column. Remove one and the picture loses a column of width without any block of it being squashed. Choose the seam that carries the least detail, and the parts a viewer cares about survive while the empty parts pay the cost.

“Least detail” is made concrete by an energy map: the absolute gradient at every pixel, which a single Conv with a Sobel pair produces in one call. The cheapest seam is then found by dynamic programming. Sweeping down the rows, each cell records the total energy of the best path reaching it, which is its own energy plus the smallest of the three cells above it. The last row holds the cost of every complete seam, and the smallest entry is traced back upward to recover the path.

Measured here on an 80×256 picture: the figure spans 25 columns by construction, and after 77 seams are removed it still spans 25. A plain resize to the same final width leaves it 17 columns wide, exactly the round(25 × 179/256) that uniform scaling predicts. The traced seam's energy matches the value the dynamic program left in its final row to a relative error of 7.7e-8, and no seam ever steps more than one column between adjacent rows, as the clamped-neighbour rule requires.

The cost is that the row sweep is inherently serial: 564 kernel dispatches per seam, and the 77 seams run at 0.22 seams per second here. The card does each row's arithmetic at once, but the rows must still be walked one after another.

The picture is one [1,4,H,W] tensor: three colour channels and a fourth channel that marks the figure, carried along so the page can measure it afterwards. Energy is one Conv with a [2,4,3,3] weight — a Sobel pair on luminance, with zero weight on the marker channel — then Abs, a Reshape, a ReduceSum over the two gradient channels and a Reshape back. The dynamic program is seven calls per row: two Gather calls that shift the previous row left and right with clamped edges, a Concat that stacks the three candidates, a ReduceMin down the stack, a Gather for this row of the energy, an Add, and a Concat that appends the row to the accumulated map. One Concat then brings the energy map and the cumulative map back in a single readback, because the backtrack is serial. Deletion is a Reshape to one long vector, a single Gather with a flat index list that skips one pixel per row, and a Reshape back.
