An untrained neural network as a painting machine

Every pixel's coordinates go into a small network with random weights that nobody trained. The three outputs are read as red, green and blue. Re-roll the seed for a different painting. The first layer is a million-row matrix multiply at 1024×1024, and it is a thin, wide shape, so it does far less arithmetic per row than a square one.

The science

There is no training here and no data. The network is a function from a pixel's coordinates to a colour, and the picture is that function drawn over the plane. Everything interesting comes from the shape of the function, not from anything it learned.

The first layer is what makes it look like anything at all. It computes sin(f · Wx) for a random matrix W, which is the random Fourier feature construction: a set of sinusoids at random frequencies and random orientations. Rahimi and Recht showed that averaging such features approximates a smooth stationary kernel, and the practical consequence here is that the frequency slider f sets a single length scale. The typical distance between features in the image falls roughly as 1/f, so a low f gives broad washes and a high f gives fine grain. Without that layer, tanh of a linear function of x and y can only produce soft diagonal ramps.

The tanh layers that follow compose those sinusoids. A composition of smooth bounded functions stays smooth and bounded, so the output has no edges except where a tanh saturates — and a saturating tanh is exactly what draws a hard contour. Feeding in the radius r = √(x2+y2) alongside x and y adds a term with rotational symmetry, which is why concentric structure appears so often. The final sigmoid maps each of the three outputs into [0, 1] to be read as red, green and blue.

Input per pixel: x, y in [−1, 1] and the distance from the centre. Layer 1 is Sin of a random projection, scaled by the frequency slider. The hidden layers are MatMul, Add, Tanh. The output is Sigmoid. Everything after the coordinate upload stays on the card until the final colour readback.
