The science

The fluid follows Stam's stable method, which splits one step into moving the fluid along its own velocity, then correcting that velocity so it neither creates nor destroys volume. The correction is the expensive half. It requires solving a Poisson equation, and it is solved here by Jacobi iteration: repeatedly replacing every cell with the average of its four neighbours plus a source term. Each sweep is one Conv call.

Jacobi's behaviour is exactly predictable, and the page checks itself against that prediction before any animation begins. Zero padding in Conv acts as a fixed boundary, which makes a sine field an exact eigenvector of the discrete Laplacian. For such a field, each sweep multiplies the leftover error by precisely cos(πj/(N+1)), where j counts how many oscillations the field has across the grid. After 20 sweeps the error must therefore be that factor raised to the twentieth power.

Measured on a 128² grid, against theory: the smooth mode j=4 leaves 0.9093321970 where theory says 0.9093321930; the middle mode j=32 leaves 0.0011022035 against 0.0011022045; the roughest mode j=120 leaves 0.6161289335 against 0.6161289248. All three agree to within about 1e-6 of the predicted ratio.

The shape of those three numbers is the lesson. Jacobi almost eliminates the middle frequency and barely touches either the smoothest or the roughest, so the fluid is never truly free of divergence, only free of the part Jacobi is good at removing. Set the sweep count to zero and the pressure is identically zero, the correction subtracts nothing, and the retained divergence reads exactly 1.

Per step, in order. A Gaussian source: Sub and Mul build the squared distance to the pointer, Exp turns it into a blob, and Mul, Concat and Add push velocity and dye into the field. Advection: Transpose lays the velocity out as sampling coordinates, Mul and Add step every cell centre backwards down the flow, and two GridSample calls read the velocity and the dye from where each parcel came from. Projection: one Conv takes the divergence with a forward difference, Mul and Concat pack the right-hand side into a second channel beside the pressure so that a single 2×2×3×3 Conv is one whole Jacobi sweep, and a last Conv plus Sub subtracts a backward-difference gradient. Forward difference in and backward difference out compose into exactly the five-point Laplacian the sweeps solve, which is why the two halves of the projection agree. Pressure starts from zero every step, so the slider is the entire solve. Two more Conv calls measure the divergence that survived and the vorticity, and one Concat packs dye, divergence and curl into a single readback. That is 28 + K calls a step for K sweeps, and only the last one leaves the card.
